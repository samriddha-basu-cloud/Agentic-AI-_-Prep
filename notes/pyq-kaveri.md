## A1 — Model answer (2 marks)

### (a) Two claims I would not rely on as stated
- **Claim 3 (fill "averages 73.7%", "market-wide") — hides a trend inside an average.** The 73.7% is a simple average of five falling months (86.0 → 58.2%); it conceals five consecutive declines and a current fill of 58.2%, and "market-wide" is asserted without evidence while Kaveri's own RDCs held 662 unsold cars.
- **Claim 2 ("Month 4 proved the operating model self-corrects") — cherry-picks its month.** M4's profit came from a forced production cut (chip allocation: production cost fell from ₹26.4 cr to ₹16.8 cr), not from any correction; fill fell to 64.5% and cash stayed negative, and the "near break-even" M5 hides ≈₹11 cr of forgone contribution (A2b).
- *(Also defective, and acceptable as a second pick:)* **Claim 1 — reports activity as outcome** (L4 foundations and "six capabilities" vs. only one of three live agents hitting its objective, one routing agent net −₹2.6 L, one repricing agent below gate, the one agent that fixes placement NOT BUILT; and only 4 capabilities are actually built or building, not six). **Claim 4 — argues insurance logic backwards** (rail is treated as wasted money because "road has always sufficed", in the very month road failed: ₹60 L congestion charge with 0% cover).

### (b) Exposing Claim 3 with the arithmetic
| | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fill % | 86.0 | 85.9 | 73.8 | 64.5 | **58.2** |
| Lost sales | 71 | 65 | 92 | 188 | **269** |

- Simple average = 368.4 ÷ 5 = **73.7%** — correct arithmetic, misleading statistic.
- Volume-weighted season fill = 1,807 ÷ 2,492 = **72.5%** — lower still, because the bad months carried more demand (M5 demand 643 vs M1 506).
- The *current* run-rate is **58.2%**, i.e. **15.5 points below** the quoted average, after **five consecutive declines**; lost sales rose **3.8×** (71 → 269).
- "Shortfall months were market-wide" is contradicted by Kaveri's own stock: in M5 it lost 269 sales **while holding 662 finished cars in its RDCs** — that is a placement failure inside Kaveri, not a market-wide shortage.

*(Alternative exposure of Claim 2:* M4 production cost ₹16.8 cr vs ₹26.4 cr in M3 = **−₹9.6 cr**, forced by the 462-chip-set allocation ("production scaled to match"). Revenue 33.8 − production 16.8 − overheads ≈13.4 = **+₹3.6 cr** — the profit is a by-product of producing less, while fill fell 9.3 points to 64.5% and cash was still −₹11.5 cr. One month later the "model" lost money again.)*

### (c) The load-bearing item to verify first
**Claim 4 — the month-6 materials order (245 HATCH · 105 SUV · 65 EV = 415 cars) and "no rail for month 6".** It is the only forward-looking commitment in the memo: it locks month-6 cash and capacity **before** month 6 runs (rail must be booked a month ahead; chip fees are charged on the full book), and it was "booked against the month-5 demand plan" without netting the **727 finished cars** already in plant yards and RDCs. If it is wrong, month 6 repeats month 5 — more unrouted stock, road-only, 0% cover — so verify it against zone-level demand and current stock before anything else.

## A2 — Model answer (3 marks)

**Assumptions:** A1. The 269 lost sales had the **same model mix as the 374 cars sold in M5** (219 HATCH · 96 SUV · 59 EV, i.e. 58.6% / 25.7% / 15.8%). A2. **Contribution per car = month-5 price − unit production cost**; freight, storage and fixed overheads are excluded because they are largely fixed and not itemised. A3. The lost sales were genuine demand that would have bought at month-5 prices (no further discount needed). A4. The 662 RDC cars were saleable models, so the failure is location (and partly mix), not quality. A5. ₹1 crore = 100 lakh.

### (a) How 662 cars in RDCs and 269 lost sales can both be true
Under the game's physics a dealer zone can **only pull from its own region's RDC**, and any plant stock that is **not routed** by a plan **auto-pushes by road to the plant's home RDC** (the default lane). Kaveri never filed a distribution plan-of-record (the **Distribution Planning Agent is NOT BUILT**) and booked **zero rail** all season, so in M5 **276 cars** were pushed to the plants' home RDCs regardless of where demand was. Stock therefore piled up in the RDCs **near the plants** (RDC stock 226 → 165 → 330 → 556 → **662**) while zones served by **other** RDCs ran dry; there is no automatic cross-RDC rebalancing, and corridor congestion in M5 stranded what expedited moves were attempted (₹60 L). Aggregate stock was high; **stock in the right region and mix was low**. It is a **placement failure, not a supply failure** — exactly the Mode & Routing ledger's diagnosis ("₹4.1 L missed on lanes where **no planned flows existed**").

### (b) Contribution forgone on the 269 lost sales
| Model | Share of M5 sales | Lost units (269 × share) | Unit contribution (price − cost) | Contribution forgone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HATCH | 219/374 = 58.6% | 157.5 | 6.05 − 3.85 = ₹2.20 L | 157.5 × 2.20 = **₹346.5 L** |
| SUV | 96/374 = 25.7% | 69.0 | 15.50 − 8.30 = ₹7.20 L | 69.0 × 7.20 = **₹497.1 L** |
| EV | 59/374 = 15.8% | 42.4 | 14.05 − 8.00 = ₹6.05 L | 42.4 × 6.05 = **₹256.7 L** |
| **Total** | | **269** | weighted avg ₹4.09 L/car | **≈ ₹1,100 L = ₹11.0 cr** |

*(Shortcut: weighted contribution per car = (219×2.20 + 96×7.20 + 59×6.05) ÷ 374 = 1,529.95 ÷ 374 = ₹4.09 L; × 269 = ₹1,100 L.)*

**Set against the reported figures:**
- Forgone contribution **₹11.0 cr ≈ 5.8× the reported loss of ₹1.9 cr** — had those 269 sales been made, M5 would have shown roughly **+₹9.1 cr** (−1.9 + 11.0) instead of "near break-even". The memo's "held near break-even" hides an ₹11 cr hole.
- It is **≈ 18× the ₹60 L congestion charge** (1,100 ÷ 60 = 18.3). The visible charge is the small cost; the invisible one (lost contribution) is the big one.
- And because the cars **already existed** in RDCs, the true cash forgone is closer to the **revenue** on those sales: 269 × ₹9.74 L avg price = **≈ ₹26.2 cr** — almost double the month-end cash deficit of ₹13.3 cr. Selling the stock it already has is Kaveri's biggest cash lever.

### (c) Cheapest standing change that stops the pattern recurring
**File a monthly distribution plan-of-record — commission the Distribution Planning Agent (2 tokens; its D2·P1·T1 gate is already met by L4 foundations, so it delivers full effect) — so that every car leaving a plant is routed against the zone-level demand plan and the default auto-push ends, with a standing rail floor booked a month ahead on the planned lanes.** Cost: 2 tokens (one-off) plus rail book-ahead charges that are ~25% cheaper than road; benefit at stake: up to ₹11 cr of contribution a month. It also gives the existing Mode & Routing agent the planned flows it was missing.

## B1 — Model answer (5 marks)

**Assumptions:** A1. The binding constraint is **placement, not supply or price** — 727 finished cars exist while 269 sales were lost in M5 (A2). A2. The month-5 demand plan is broadly right at zone level (Forecast Error/Bias Tracking is the one capability **hitting its objective**), so placing stock against it will convert into sales. A3. The Distribution Planning Agent's gate (D2·P1·T1) is met by Kaveri's L4 foundations, so it ships at full designed effect and is not subject to below-gate build odds. A4. Rail slots booked a month ahead are ~25% cheaper than road and charged whether used or not; a floor sized to the demand plan's base load will be mostly used. A5. The repricing agent lands in M7 regardless (I am not choosing option ii), so its upkeep is a cost I accept for two months. A6. Monthly token budget stays at 24.

### The choice: **(i) distribution plan-of-record**
It attacks the largest pool on the page (₹11.0 cr/month of forgone contribution, or ≈₹26 cr of revenue on cars already built), and it is also the **best cash move**: converting the 727 finished cars into sales stabilises cash faster than any price or chip change.

**Confronting the strongest evidence against it:** cash is −₹13.3 cr and board confidence is falling, and the exhibits show that the last distribution agent Kaveri bought (Mode & Routing) is underperforming (net −₹2.6 L) — so spending 2 more tokens plus rail book-ahead charges looks like more "transformation theatre". **My answer:** Mode & Routing failed precisely "on lanes where **no planned flows existed**" — it optimises routes but nobody gave it a plan; the Distribution Planning Agent *is* that missing plan, its gate is met (unlike the below-gate builds), and its cost (2 tokens + rail charges ~25% below road) is tiny against ₹11 cr/month lost.

**What I STOP in month 6:** letting any plant stock leave unrouted (no more default auto-push), and producing to the 415-car month-5 materials order without first netting the 727 finished cars already in the system.

### The operating mandate
| Element | Mandate |

| **Escalation trigger (control returns to humans)** | Any **capacity shock that removes more than 20% of a lane's capacity** (port jam, corridor congestion, carrier failure), **or any zone forecast to fall below 60% fill for the coming month**, **or any plan needing expedited/trailer-express spend above ₹20 L** → the agent stops executing and the CSCO re-plans (mandate card "shock trigger" 📘). |
| **Starting autonomy level** | **L3 — act with approval gates:** the agent files the plan-of-record and executes routine flows and the rail floor inside the mandate; exceptions (expedites, rail above the floor, re-plans after a shock) need CSCO approval. *Justification:* gate met and foundations at L4 make execution safe, but the agent has no track record at Kaveri and its sister routing agent underperformed — evidence must be earned before L4. |
| **Named owner** | **The CSCO (me)** is accountable for outcomes; the Head of Distribution Planning is the day-to-day agent owner. |

*(Mandate card / optimizer mandate checklist mapping 📘: service floor = protect the zones that stocked out most in M5; cost ceiling = cash constraint; mode priority = rail floor for base load, road for flex, trailer-express only by exception; shock trigger = above; review cadence = monthly plan review + day-60 gate.)*

### If you had chosen (ii) instead — what a passing answer looks like
*(Not recommended, but marks are for reasoning, so know the shape.)* Assumptions would centre on cash being the binding constraint. **Rule on the repricing agent: terminate (2 tokens)** — it is below gate (45% odds, prerequisite not live, fraction of effect), L5 closed-loop pricing on a board that cannot place stock is the wrong autonomy, and 7 tokens are sunk either way. **Right-size the chip book** to real production need (fees are charged on the full book: 515 contracted vs a 415-car materials order). **Restore prices toward list** (the cuts give away ≈₹1.5 cr/month and did not fix fill, because the shortfall is placement). Objective metric: month-end cash; constraint: fill not below 58.2%; trigger: board confidence below 90. Strongest evidence against (ii): it leaves the ₹11 cr/month placement leak untouched and does nothing about 727 idle cars — which is why (i) is the better answer.
